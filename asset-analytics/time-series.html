🎯 Executive Purpose
Statistical Time Series Diagnostics

Determine whether asset returns exhibit serial momentum persistence, mean-reversion pullbacks, calendar seasonality, or permanent long-term mathematical equilibrium with a pair asset.

📋 How to Use
2-Part Econometric Workflow

1. Autocorrelation Lab: Examine Lag 1 (1M momentum/reversion), Lag 3 (quarterly earnings cycles), and Lag 12 (annual seasonality).
2. Cointegration Test: Test pairs stationarity using the Engle-Granger Augmented Dickey-Fuller (ADF) test.
3. Spread Trading: Track residual Z-scores across standard deviation bands for statistical mean-reversion rebalancing.

🔍 What to Expect
Stationarity vs Random Walks

• Asset Prices: Generally non-stationary random walks I(1) with drifting means.
• Cointegrated Pairs: Linear combinations that form stationary I(0) residuals with bounded variance and predictable half-lives.
• Regime Shifts: Rolling autocorrelation detects when trend-following persistence breaks down into range-bound chop.

💡 Executive Output Decoder
Econometric Thresholds

• ADF t-Stat < -3.37: Confirms cointegration at 95% confidence (rejects unit root).
• p-Value < 0.05: Statistically significant mean-reverting relationship.
• Spread Z-Score ±2σ: Statistically extreme divergence signaling high-probability reversion opportunities.

⚓ Cointegration vs Correlation
The Invisible Leash

Correlation measures temporary co-movement. Cointegration proves two prices share a permanent long-term mathematical equilibrium.

⚖️ Hedge Ratio (β)
Optimal Basket Multiplier

OLS regression slope determining the portfolio spread: Spread = Y − β×X. Isolates the stationary residual.

📉 ADF Test Statistic
Unit Root Rejection

Augmented Dickey-Fuller t-statistic must be more negative than the 95% critical value (-3.37) to confirm mean-reverting stationarity.

🎯 p-Value (< 0.05)
Pairs Trading & Rebalancing

Statistically significant p-values mean temporary price spreads reliably snap back, enabling low-risk mean-reversion rebalancing.
