🎯 Executive Purpose
Dynamic Volatility Budgeting

Financial risk is non-constant: volatility clusters in violent bursts during market panics, followed by long periods of calm compounding. Volatility targeting caps total portfolio volatility at a specified threshold, de-risking automatically before crashes deepen.

📋 How to Use
Dynamic Leverage Scaling

1. Target Volatility: Set risk budget (e.g. 10% or 12% annualized vol).
2. Measure Trailing Volatility: Compute realized volatility of US Equities over trailing 3 months ($\sigma_{\text{realized}}$).
3. Scale Weight: Set equity weight $w = \min(1.0, \sigma_{\text{target}} / \sigma_{\text{realized}})$ and allocate remainder $(1 - w)$ to Cash.

🔍 What to Expect
Regime Shift Dynamics

• Calm Bull Expansions: When market volatility drops to 8–12%, holds 100% equity exposure.
• Crisis Spikes (2008, 2020): Realized volatility spikes to 30–50%; model rapidly cuts equity weight to ~25–35%, moving into cash.
• Crash Cushioning: Slashes portfolio drawdown severity during market freefalls.

💡 Executive Output Decoder
Risk Stabilization & Sharpe Ratio

• Tightly Controlled Risk: Achieves realized volatility close to budget (~11.9% vs target 10.0%), eliminating panic selling.
• Enhanced Sharpe Ratio: Eliminating catastrophic negative returns improves overall capital efficiency.
• Ideal for Decumulation: Prevents sequence-of-returns drawdown shocks during retirement distribution phase.
